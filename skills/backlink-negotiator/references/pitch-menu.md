# Pitch menu

Options to offer a site owner instead of, or alongside, cash — and what tends to land where. Only offer what step 1 of the skill actually confirmed the product can deliver; don't invent a testimonial program or a comp-account tier that doesn't exist.

## The options

**Testimonial or case study** — a quote, review, or short write-up from the product about using the site/service, or about the outcome of working with the owner directly. Costs the product little, and gives the site owner content they can use themselves. Works well when the site owner runs a tool, service, or community the product's team could genuinely vouch for.

**Reciprocal backlink** — a link back from the product's own site or a relevant piece of content. Only offer this where it's a legitimate, on-topic placement for the product's own site — a reciprocal link that only exists to close the deal (irrelevant to the product's own content) can look manipulative to both search engines and readers, and isn't worth the compromise. If the product doesn't have a genuinely relevant place to link back, don't manufacture one.

**Comped or free product access** — a free account, extended trial, or upgraded tier. Effective for resource pages/roundups aimed at an audience who might actually use the product, and for review/comparison sites where hands-on access improves the piece.

**Social share / newsletter mention** — promoting the site owner's content or site through the product's own channels. Low cost, best used as an add-on alongside another offer rather than the sole thing on the table, unless the product has real distribution (a newsletter/audience the owner would value).

**Co-marketing** — a joint piece of content, webinar, or cross-promotion. Higher effort for both sides; reserve this for high-fit sites where an ongoing relationship is worth more than a single link.

**Guest content** — offering to write the piece (or a section of it) yourself, saving the site owner the effort. Fits editorial sites and blogs that publish contributed content, less so directories or resource-page-style listings that don't run articles.

**Data, quote, or original research** — a stat, survey result, or expert quote the site owner can cite in their piece. Works well for journalists, roundup writers, and anyone assembling a list-style or research-backed article, where original data is something they need anyway.

## Matching pitch to site type

- **Resource pages / roundups** — reciprocal link, comped access, or a data point/quote tend to land best; these owners are usually curating for their readers, not looking for a relationship.
- **Directories** — usually just want the listing filled out correctly; rarely worth more than confirming details unless they're charging for a featured/verified slot, in which case weigh the fee against the rubric.
- **Editorial sites / blogs** — testimonials, guest content, and co-marketing fit better here since there's an actual byline and ongoing publication to work with.
- **Sites that explicitly ask for payment** — a non-cash offer can sometimes substitute or reduce the ask, but don't force it; some site owners run this as a straightforward line of revenue and won't accept anything else. That's fine to walk away from per the rubric.

## Combining offers

It's fine to lead with the strongest single offer rather than listing everything at once — a menu of five options in one email reads as unfocused. Pick the one or two most likely to matter to this specific owner, and mention that there's flexibility if it's not quite right.
