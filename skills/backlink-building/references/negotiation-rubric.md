# Negotiation rubric

A framework for turning a fit assessment + an owner's ask into a recommendation. Use it as a way of thinking, not a formula to plug numbers into — the reasoning matters more than the label.

## The two axes

**Fit** — how much a link from this specific page would actually help this specific product. High fit means real topical/audience overlap, decent editorial quality, a placement type that's likely to be seen and clicked (not just crawled). Low fit means thin relevance, weak or spammy editorial signals, or a placement type that's mostly there for link volume (e.g. an unmoderated directory).

**Ask** — what the owner wants in return, and how that cost compares to what a link from this page is actually worth. A $30 fee for a well-trafficked, tightly relevant resource page is a different proposition than the same $30 for a low-traffic aggregator. A request for a reciprocal link often costs nothing but consider whether the swap page will devalue the user's own site (linking out to something irrelevant purely to get a link back is a bad trade even when it's "free").

## How they combine

- **High fit + reasonable/free ask (reciprocal link, testimonial, guest post with editorial control retained by them)** → generally accept. This is close to what the placement is worth anyway.
- **High fit + payment or a costly ask** → worth countering. The site is valuable enough to negotiate rather than walk away from; try to bring the cost down or substitute a non-cash offer from the pitch menu before agreeing to pay.
- **Low fit + any paid ask** → generally decline. Don't spend money (or free product) buying a link that won't move the needle for this specific product, no matter how cheap.
- **Low fit + free/reciprocal ask** → usually still decline or deprioritize. A reciprocal link isn't actually free if the source page has no real audience — it's still a wasted outbound link on the user's side for a placement nobody will read.
- **Ambiguous fit (real content, unclear audience overlap)** → lean on the reply thread itself. How the owner writes about the ask (do they seem to run a genuine audience, do they reference real traffic/analytics, are they selective about what they link to) is itself a fit signal.

## Judgment calls worth flagging explicitly to the user

- Any ask involving payment above a token amount — the user may have their own budget ceiling that isn't visible from the data.
- Any site where fit is borderline and the ask is non-trivial (a paid guest post, exclusivity terms, no-follow-only policy).
- Any reply that reads as a mass-templated "we monetize our links" auto-response rather than a genuine negotiation — worth a quick decline rather than investing pitch effort.

## What "decline" should look like

Declining doesn't have to mean going silent. A brief, polite pass (rather than ghosting) keeps the relationship open for a better-fit opportunity later and costs little to send.
